# Bonus 2 — Credit scoring: Equifax, Experian and TransUnion

**Status:** working draft for the optional credit-scoring bonus. This is separate from the Asset Management Calculation Excel exercise.

## What is missing from the current draft?

The three mission statements are a useful introduction, but they do not yet constitute a complete comparison. The bonus should distinguish:

- a **credit bureau / credit reporting agency**, which collects and supplies credit information;
- a **credit report**, which contains reported information about a borrower;
- a **credit score**, which is produced by a scoring model using data from one or more sources;
- a **lender decision**, which also includes affordability, policy, collateral, regulation and human or automated review.

Equifax, Experian and TransUnion should therefore not be presented simply as three identical “score makers”. Their data, products, reporting coverage and update timing may differ, and lenders may use different scoring models.

## Suggested five-slide structure

### Slide 1 — What is a credit bureau?

A credit bureau collects, organizes and supplies information used in credit, fraud and identity decisions. A credit report is not the same thing as a credit score: the report contains information, while a scoring model converts selected information into a risk indicator. The final lending decision belongs to the lender.

### Slide 2 — Three companies, three emphases

| Company | Public positioning to verify | Main emphasis for the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Equifax | Data, analytics and technology supporting decisions and financial wellbeing | Credit information, decisioning, identity and fraud solutions |
| Experian | Global data and technology supporting business decisions and consumer financial goals | Credit risk, decisioning, fraud prevention, consumer services and financial inclusion |
| TransUnion | “Information for Good” and a reliable representation of people in commerce | Trust, economic opportunity, risk, fraud and consumer empowerment |

The mission statements are positioning statements. They should be complemented by concrete products and use cases.

### Slide 3 — How the information flows into a bank decision

1. A consumer or business applies for credit.
2. Lenders and other data furnishers report information to a bureau.
3. The bureau stores, matches and supplies a credit report or data product.
4. A scoring model produces a score or risk indicator.
5. The lender combines the information with income, affordability, collateral, policy and regulation.
6. The result may be approval, rejection, pricing, a limit decision or a request for further information.

### Slide 4 — What the companies provide

Compare the following categories rather than only their slogans:

- consumer and business credit reports;
- credit scores and decisioning tools;
- identity verification and fraud prevention;
- monitoring, alerts and dispute processes;
- analytics, risk management and portfolio monitoring;
- consumer education and financial-health tools.

The same category does not necessarily mean the same data coverage, methodology, market or customer base.

### Slide 5 — Limits and conclusion

Credit information improves risk assessment, but it is not a complete measure of a person's ability or willingness to repay. Important limits include incomplete or incorrect data, reporting delays, different scoring models, regional regulation, privacy, potential bias and limited transparency for consumers.

**Conclusion:** all three companies use information and technology to support credit and risk decisions, but their positioning differs: Equifax emphasizes data-driven decisions and financial wellbeing, Experian combines data and decisioning with consumer financial services, and TransUnion frames its activity around trust and “Information for Good”.

## Short conclusion for the submission

Equifax, Experian and TransUnion are major credit-information and data-analytics companies, but they should not be confused with a single universal credit-score model. Their information can support lending, fraud prevention and monitoring, while the final decision depends on the lender, the scoring model, the data available and the applicable regulation.
