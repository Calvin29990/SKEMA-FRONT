## Slide 9 — Securitization: how bank credit becomes a market instrument

### Slide objective

Explain the financial mechanism from loan origination to investor cash flows. The key question is not only “what is securitization?” but also:

> **Who receives the cash flows, who absorbs the losses and who keeps the residual risks?**

### Recommended visual flow

```text
Borrowers
    ↓ loan repayments
Bank originates and services a loan pool
    ↓ true sale or structured transfer
Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)
    ↓ issues securities
Senior / mezzanine / equity tranches
    ↓ cash-flow waterfall
Investors
```

### Main concepts to show

1. **Origination** — the bank creates a portfolio of loans to households, consumers or companies.
2. **Pooling** — individual loans are grouped so that investors receive exposure to a portfolio rather than to one borrower.
3. **SPV** — a separate vehicle holds or references the assets and issues notes to investors.
4. **Tranching** — the portfolio's cash flows and losses are allocated by priority.
5. **Waterfall** — collected cash is distributed first to senior claims, then to mezzanine claims, then to the equity or residual tranche.
6. **Credit enhancement** — depending on the structure, protection may include subordination, overcollateralisation, excess spread, reserve accounts or liquidity facilities.

### Tranche logic

| Tranche | Payment priority | Loss absorption | Investor profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Senior | First | Last | Lower expected risk and lower spread |
| Mezzanine | After senior | Before equity | Intermediate risk and spread |
| Equity / residual | Last | First | Highest risk and residual return |

### Key sentence for the slide

> **Securitization does not make credit risk disappear: it transforms, redistributes and reprices the risk across legal entities, tranches and investors.**

### Speaker note

The bank may obtain funding or reduce a particular exposure, but it can still retain risks through servicing obligations, retained tranches, representations and warranties, liquidity commitments, reputation effects or future origination incentives. Do not present securitization as an automatic transfer of every risk away from the bank.

## Slide 10 — Front-office view: structuring, pricing, distribution and risk

### Slide objective

Show how the same mechanism is read by a structurer, a sales professional, a trader or a credit investor.

### Deal lifecycle

```text
1. Origination
2. Pool selection and due diligence
3. SPV and tranche structuring
4. Credit enhancement and documentation
5. Pricing and investor distribution
6. Servicing, monitoring and hedging
```

### Sales perspective

A sales professional asks:

- What investor mandate fits the senior, mezzanine or equity tranche?
- What yield, spread, rating and liquidity does the investor require?
- Can the risk profile be explained clearly to the client?
- What are the key sensitivities: defaults, recoveries, prepayments, rates and liquidity?
- Is the product suitable for the investor's risk limits and investment policy?

### Trading / markets perspective

A trader or markets professional monitors:

- credit-spread risk and mark-to-market movements;
- interest-rate and duration exposure;
- prepayment or extension risk;
- basis and liquidity risk;
- inventory, hedging costs and secondary-market demand;
- model risk in the cash-flow and tranche assumptions.

### Credit-risk bridge

```text
Expected Loss ≈ Probability of Default × Loss Given Default × Exposure at Default
```

Use this only as a simple risk-management framework. The securitisation structure then determines how losses are allocated between the tranches.

### Why 2008 matters

The crisis lesson was not that securitization alone was the cause. The danger came from the combination of:

- weak underwriting;
- complex and opaque structures;
- excessive leverage;
- over-reliance on ratings;
- misaligned incentives;
- underestimated default correlation;
- dependence on market liquidity.

### Contemporary relevance

The same banking-business questions remain relevant today:

- Is the underlying credit genuinely understood?
- Is the risk transferred, retained or only transformed?
- Are capital, liquidity and stress scenarios adequate?
- Can investors price the risk transparently?
- Are incentives aligned between originator, arranger, distributor and investor?

### Key sentence for the slide

> **For the front office, securitization is not only a funding technique: it is a structuring, pricing, distribution and risk-governance exercise.**

## What to avoid

- Do not turn the slides into a detailed derivatives presentation.
- Do not state that securitization caused the 2008 crisis by itself.
- Do not claim that senior tranches are risk-free.
- Do not claim that a rating is the same as a probability of default.
- Do not invent a transaction size, default rate or return without a cited case.
- Do not add Hull unless the exact title, edition and page are available; the two slides are already sufficiently rigorous without it.
