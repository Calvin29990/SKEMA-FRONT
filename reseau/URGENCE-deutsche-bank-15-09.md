mauvaise trace. Une bonne question a **quatre** propriétés :

1. Elle prouve que **tu as lu les résultats** (chiffre à l'appui)

## 4.2 Les 3 réponses à savoir dire par cœur (90 secondes chacune)

### ① *"Tell me about yourself."* — la seule que tu dois réciter mot pour mot

> "I'm Calvin, a finance student in Paris focusing on market finance. What got
> me into trading was less the markets in the abstract and more a concrete
> thing: I built a small lab where I model a geopolitical shock on oil and
> measure the P&L of a hedged book on real data. What I learned from it is that
> **timing matters more than direction** — in my own numbers, exiting at the
> peak versus holding to a stop was worth about six hundred thousand dollars on
> a twenty-five million book. That's what pulled me towards a trading desk
> rather than research. Right now I'm working through rates and derivatives
> fundamentals, and I'm here because Deutsche Bank is the one European FIC
> franchise that competes globally rather than regionally."

**Pourquoi ça marche :** un fait concret, un chiffre avec provenance, une leçon,
une raison d'être là. **Pas de liste d'adjectifs.**

### ② *"Why Deutsche Bank?"*

> "Two reasons. First, positioning: DB is the number one European FIC house and
> the strategy is explicitly to be the global alternative to US banks —
> that's a different bet from most European players who are consolidating
> regionally. Second, the mix: FIC revenues were 9.6 billion euros in 2025, up
> 13 percent, and the last quarter was a record. But what interests me is that
> management describes the franchise as **diversified enough not to depend on
> volatility**. That's the harder thing to build, and it's what I'd want to
> learn on a desk."

### ③ *"Why rates / why FIC?"*

> "Rates is where macro becomes a tradeable price. Right now the ECB has just
> hiked to two and a half percent on the deposit rate, French OAT-Bund is
> around eighty-five basis points, and every one of those numbers is a position
> someone has to hold and hedge. I like that the instrument is simple — a bond,
> a swap — but the risk is not: duration, convexity, curve, basis. And unlike
> equities, you can't hand-wave it, the cash flows are contractual."

## 4.3 Le protocole du mock call (30–40 min avec Yann ou Ali)

**Envoie-lui ce format à l'avance** — un contact accepte beaucoup plus
facilement quand la demande est cadrée :

```
1.  (5 min)  Je me présente en anglais — tu m'interromps si c'est confus
2. (10 min)  3-4 questions techniques rates, en anglais
3.  (5 min)  Je pitche une vue de marché — 2 minutes chrono
4. (10 min)  Ton feedback : ce qui ne passerait pas sur un desk
5.  (5 min)  Tes conseils pour le Spotlight du 15
```

**Les questions techniques à lui demander de te poser** (elles collent à ton J1
et à l'event) :

1. *What's the difference between a forward and a future?*
2. *Duration and convexity — explain them to a client in thirty seconds.*
3. *The curve steepens. What does that tell you, and how do you trade it?*
4. *What is a repo, and why would a bond trade special?*
5. *Why does a call option get more expensive when volatility rises?*

**Les 4 premières sont dans ton programme (J13–J18).** Si tu ne sais pas
répondre, **dis-le en anglais** — c'est un mock, c'est fait pour ça :
> *"I don't know that one yet — it's on my list for next week. Can you walk me
> through how you'd answer it?"*

**Cette phrase est un atout, pas un aveu.** Elle montre que tu sais dire « je ne
sais pas » sans t'effondrer — exactement ce qu'on teste en Superday.
