# 50 questions d'entretien FIC — FR / EN

**À lire avant chaque entretien.** Du plus simple au plus complexe.

> 🎯 **Comment lire.** Chaque question a : la **réponse anglaise** (à dire tel
> quel, anglais simple et pro), le **rappel FR** (pourquoi c'est vrai), et un
> **focus** quand la réponse contient un piège. L'anglais est volontairement
> simple : **on juge la clarté, pas le vocabulaire rare**.

**Sommaire :** Bloc A actions (1-6) · B taux (7-14) · C obligations (15-22) ·
D dérivés fermes (23-28) · E options (29-38) · F vol (39-42) ·
G crédit & FX (43-46) · H structuration (47-50) · **I ton profil (51-60)**

# BLOC A — Actions (1-6)

### 1. What is a stock?
> "A share of ownership in a company. You get a claim on its profits, usually
> through dividends, and you take the risk of losing your capital."

**FR :** part de propriété. Tu es **actionnaire**, dernier servi en cas de
faillite — d'où le rendement attendu plus élevé.

### 2. What drives a share price?
> "Expected future cash flows, discounted back. So two things move it: the
> **expectations** themselves, and the **rate** you discount them at. That's why
> equities fall when rates rise, all else equal."

> 💡 **Focus.** Cette réponse est parfaite parce qu'elle **relie actions et
> taux** dès la question 2. Un candidat qui dit juste « l'offre et la demande »
> passe pour un débutant.

### 3. What is a dividend?
> "Part of the profit paid out to shareholders. On the ex-dividend date, the
> share price drops by roughly the dividend amount — no free money."

### 4. What is market cap?
> "Share price times number of shares. It's the market's valuation of the
> equity, not of the whole company — for that you'd add net debt to get
> enterprise value."

### 5. What is an index?
> "A basket tracking a market — the S&P 500, the CAC 40. Most are
> **market-cap weighted**, so the biggest companies drive most of the move."

### 6. Long or short — what's the difference?
> "Long means I own it and I profit if it goes up. Short means I sold something
> I borrowed, so I profit if it falls — but my loss is **unlimited**, because
> there's no ceiling on a price."

**Vocabulaire A :** *a share / a stock · a dividend · market cap · enterprise
value · to go long / short · unlimited downside*

# BLOC B — Taux (7-14)

### 7. What is discounting?
> "Money today is worth more than money tomorrow. At four percent, a hundred
> euros in one year is worth **96.15 today** — because 96.15 invested today
> gives you a hundred."

**FR :** en 10 ans, ces 100 € ne valent plus que **67,56 €**. Plus c'est loin,
moins ça vaut.

### 8. Simple vs compound interest?
> "Simple pays on the principal only. Compound pays on interest already earned.
> Over long horizons the difference is huge."

**Rappel :** règle de 72 → à 6 %, un capital double en **12 ans** (exact : 11,9).

### 9. What is the yield curve?
> "It's the price of time — the rate demanded for each maturity. Normally it
> slopes up, because lending longer is riskier."

### 10. What does an inverted curve mean?
> "Short rates above long rates. It usually means the market expects the central
> bank to **cut** — often because a slowdown is coming. It's one of the most
> watched recession signals."

### 11. Steepening or flattening?
> "Steepening means the gap between short and long widens. Flattening means it
> narrows. A **bear flattener** — short rates rising faster — is typical of a
> central bank tightening."

> 💡 **Focus.** *Bull* veut dire **prix qui montent**, donc **taux qui
> baissent**. C'est contre-intuitif et c'est exactement pour ça qu'on le
> demande. Ne l'inverse pas.

### 12. What is a basis point?
> "One hundredth of a percent. We say **a bip**. On ten million, one bip is a
> thousand euros a year — never negligible."

### 13. What did the ECB just do?
> "They hiked twenty-five bips, taking the deposit rate to **2.50 percent**. The
> market is pricing roughly a **forty percent** chance of another hike in
> December."

> ⚠️ **Vérifie ce chiffre le matin même** sur Yahoo Finance. Une donnée périmée
> fait plus de mal que pas de donnée.

### 14. Nominal vs real rate?
> "Real is nominal minus inflation. If I earn three percent while inflation runs
> at three, I've earned nothing in purchasing power."

**Vocabulaire B :** *to discount · the yield curve · steepening / flattening ·
a hike / a cut · a basis point (a bip) · to price in · tightening / easing*

# BLOC C — Obligations (15-22)

### 15. What is a bond?
> "A loan in tradable form. I lend a hundred, I receive a coupon every year, and
> I get the principal back at maturity."

### 16. ⭐ Why do bond prices fall when yields rise?
> "Because my old bond becomes less attractive. If I hold a three percent bond
> and the market now offers four, nobody buys mine at par — I have to **drop the
> price** until the buyer gets the equivalent of four percent."

**Le chiffre :** obligation 5 ans, coupon 3 % → à 3 % elle vaut **100**, à 4 %
**95,55**, à 5 % **91,34**.

> 💡 **Focus.** **C'est LA question du bloc FIC**, celle qui élimine. Elle est
> parfaite parce que tu **expliques le mécanisme** au lieu de réciter « c'est
> inversé ». Donne toujours l'exemple du 3 % contre 4 % — il rend la réponse
> impossible à confondre avec du par cœur.

### 17. What is duration?
> "The sensitivity of a bond's price to rates. A duration of five means: rates
> up one percent, price down about five percent."

**Le chiffre :** l'obligation ci-dessus a une duration modifiée de **4,53**.
Pour +100 bp, l'approximation donne **−4,53 %**, le vrai calcul **−4,40 %**.

### 18. And convexity?
> "It's the gap between that approximation and reality. The price-yield
> relationship is a **curve**, not a line — so I gain more when rates fall than
> I lose when they rise. That asymmetry is convexity, and it's why you pay for
> it."

**Le chiffre :** −100 bp → **+4,66 %** contre −100 bp → **−4,40 %**.

### 19. What is DV01?
> "The P&L for a one basis point move. On ten million with a modified duration
> of 4.53, that's about **4,530 euros per bip**. It's how a rates desk actually
> measures risk — in euros, not percentages."

> 💡 **Focus.** Répondre en **euros par bip** te fait passer d'étudiant à
> quelqu'un qui a compris comment un desk parle. Un trader ne dit jamais « ma
> duration est de 5 », il dit « je suis long 4 500 par bip ».

### 20. Clean price vs dirty price?
> "Dirty includes accrued interest, clean doesn't. Prices are quoted clean, but
> you settle dirty."

### 21. What is a repo?
> "I sell a bond and agree to buy it back tomorrow slightly more expensive. It's
> a **secured loan** — that's how a desk funds itself. If everyone wants that
> specific bond, it's **trading special** and I fund cheaper."

### 22. Why do banks hold government bonds?
> "Liquidity and regulation — they're the highest quality collateral, and they
> count towards liquidity ratios. They're also the funding backbone through
> repo."

**Vocabulaire C :** *a coupon · at par · duration · convexity · DV01 · accrued
interest · repo · trading special · collateral*

# BLOC D — Dérivés fermes (23-28)

### 23. What is a forward?
> "An agreement today to trade at a fixed price later. No cash upfront,
> everything settles at maturity."

### 24. ⭐ Is the forward a forecast of the future price?
> "**No.** It's the spot adjusted for **cost of carry** — what it costs to hold
> the asset until maturity, funding minus what the asset pays you. If gold is
> 2,000 and rates are four percent, the one-year forward is around 2,082 — not
> because gold will rise, but because you borrow to hold it."

> 💡 **Focus.** **Le piège le plus fréquent en entretien FIC.** Dire « non »
> franchement, puis expliquer le portage, te distingue immédiatement. La moitié
> des candidats répond oui.

### 25. Forward vs future?
> "Same economics. A forward is OTC and bespoke with counterparty risk. A future
> is exchange-traded, standardised, cleared, and **margined daily** — so your
> cash flows are spread over the life of the trade."

### 26. What is an interest rate swap?
> "Two parties exchange interest flows on a notional. One pays fixed, one pays
> floating. The **notional never changes hands** — only the interest does."

### 27. Why would a company enter a swap?
> "Say it borrowed floating and fears rates rising. It pays fixed and receives
> floating, so it has effectively turned floating debt into fixed debt. It
> bought **certainty**."

### 28. Paying fixed — am I long or short duration?
> "**Short duration.** I profit when rates rise. Paying fixed is economically
> like being short a bond."

> 💡 **Focus.** Question de tri classique. Le raccourci à mémoriser : **payer
> fixe = vendre une obligation**. Si tu tiens ça, tu ne te trompes jamais.

**Vocabulaire D :** *cost of carry · contango / backwardation · OTC · to clear ·
margin call · a swap · notional · fixed leg / floating leg*

# BLOC E — Options (29-38)

### 29. What is an option?
> "It's insurance. I pay a **premium** for the **right**, not the obligation, to
> buy — a call — or sell — a put — at a fixed strike."

### 30. Buyer vs seller?
> "The buyer's loss is capped at the premium, the upside is open. The seller
> collects the premium and takes **unlimited risk**. Buyers pay to sleep;
> sellers get paid to take the risk."

### 31. In the money, at the money, out of the money?
> "In the money means exercising is profitable today. At the money means the
> strike equals spot. Out of the money means it isn't worth exercising."

### 32. What is put-call parity?
> "A long call plus a short put at the same strike replicates a forward on the
> underlying. So their prices are linked by **arbitrage** — otherwise you'd lock
> in a riskless profit."

**Le chiffre :** S=100, K=100, r=4 %, q=2 %, T=0,5, C=6,20 → **P = 5,2149**

### 33. What is Black-Scholes, in one sentence?
> "It prices an option by **replication**: you can copy the option by holding a
> quantity of the underlying and adjusting it continuously. If the copy is
> perfect, there's only one possible price."

> 💡 **Focus.** Répondre « c'est une formule » est faible. Répondre
> « **réplication** » montre que tu as compris l'idée, et non appris la formule.
> C'est exactement la nuance qu'un trader écoute.

### 34. Name the assumptions. Which breaks first?
> "Lognormal returns, constant volatility, constant rates, no transaction costs,
> perfect divisibility, no arbitrage, European exercise. **Constant volatility
> breaks first** — and the proof is public: the **volatility smile**. The market
> quotes a different vol for every strike, so it contradicts the model it uses."

### 35. What is delta?
> "How much the option moves when the underlying moves. It's also the hedge
> ratio — how much underlying I hold to be neutral."

### 36. What is gamma?
> "How fast delta changes. In plain terms: **how quickly my hedge goes stale**."

### 37. ⭐ You're short gamma and the market gaps. What happens?
> "My delta moves against me. The market rallies, my delta goes short, so I have
> to **buy**. It sells off, I have to **sell**. I'm buying the highs and selling
> the lows on every re-hedge. I collect theta every day, but a fast market costs
> me more than I collect."

> 💡 **Focus.** **La meilleure question pour te démarquer.** Ajoute une phrase
> vécue : *"I saw exactly that when I back-tested the March 2020 crash on my own
> pricer."* Une expérience concrète vaut dix réponses théoriques.

### 38. Theta and gamma — the link?
> "You pay for convexity with time decay. Long gamma means I want movement and I
> pay theta for it. Short gamma means I collect theta and I want nothing to
> happen. That trade-off is the whole life of an options desk."

**Vocabulaire E :** *a premium · a strike · to exercise · in the money · delta
hedge · to go stale · time decay · convexity · to re-hedge*

# BLOC F — Volatilité (39-42)

### 39. Realised vs implied volatility?
> "Realised is what the market **did** — a calculation on past data. Implied is
> what the market **expects**, backed out of option prices."

### 40. Is implied vol a forecast?
> "**It's a price, not a forecast.** It's the vol level that makes the formula
> match the quoted price. When a trader says vol is **rich**, he means implied
> sits above what he thinks realised will be."

> 💡 **Focus.** Dire *rich* et *cheap* plutôt qu'*expensive* est le marqueur
> le plus rapide d'un candidat qui a écouté des traders parler.

### 41. Why does the smile exist?
> "Because real returns have **fat tails** — crashes happen more often than a
> normal distribution says. So out-of-the-money puts trade above model value:
> they're crash insurance. The smile is the market pricing in what the model
> leaves out."

### 42. Straddle or strangle?
> "A straddle is a call and a put at the **same strike** — I'm betting on
> movement, direction doesn't matter. A strangle uses **wider strikes**: cheaper,
> but you need a bigger move to pay off."

**Le chiffre :** straddle **24,40** contre strangle **10,69** — le straddle
coûte **2,3×** plus. C'est le prix de la zone couverte.

**Vocabulaire F :** *implied vol · realised vol · rich / cheap · fat tails ·
the smile · a straddle / a strangle · vol surface*

# BLOC G — Crédit et FX (43-46)

### 43. What is a credit spread?
> "The extra yield over the government curve — the price of default risk. A
> **150 basis point** spread on ten million is **150,000 euros a year**."

### 44. Tightening or widening?
> "Tightening means spreads narrow — the market is more confident. Widening
> means stress, and a flight to quality."

### 45. What is a CDS?
> "Insurance against default. I pay an annual premium, and I'm compensated if
> the issuer defaults. It's also how you take a credit view without owning the
> bond."

### 46. What drives an FX forward?
> "Only the **interest rate differential** — nothing about direction. If dollar
> rates are higher than euro rates, the EUR/USD forward sits above spot, exactly
> cancelling the carry. Otherwise there's an arbitrage. That's **covered
> interest parity**."

**Le chiffre :** EURUSD 1,0850, r_USD=4,25 %, r_EUR=2,25 %, 3 mois →
**F = 1,090439**, soit **+54,4 pips**.

**Vocabulaire G :** *a spread · to tighten / to widen · investment grade / high
yield · a CDS · flight to quality · a pip · carry*

# BLOC H — Structuration (47-50)

### 47. What is a structured product?
> "A packaged payoff, usually a bond plus options. A classic is capital
> protection: most of the money buys a zero-coupon bond that returns the
> principal, and the rest buys options for the upside."

> 💡 **Focus.** Cette réponse est parfaite parce qu'elle **décompose** le produit
> au lieu de le décrire. La structuration, c'est toujours **assembler des
> briques simples** — montre que tu vois les briques.

### 48. What is an autocall?
> "A note that redeems early if the underlying is above a barrier on an
> observation date. The client gets an attractive coupon, and in exchange he's
> **short a put** — he takes the downside below the barrier."

### 49. Why does a client buy a structured product rather than the option?
> "Format and access. It comes as a single security with one price, it fits in
> their mandate and accounting, and the bank handles the hedging. They're buying
> a **payoff**, not a position to manage."

### 50. Where does the bank make its money?
> "On the **spread between the payoff sold and the cost of hedging it**. The
> desk replicates the structure with vanilla instruments and keeps the
> difference — which is why pricing the hedge properly is the whole job."

> 💡 **Focus.** Réponse honnête et lucide. Beaucoup de candidats disent « des
> frais ». Dire **spread de couverture** montre que tu comprends le métier.

**Vocabulaire H :** *a payoff · capital protection · a zero-coupon · a barrier ·
an autocall · to unwind · vanilla vs exotic · to replicate*

# BLOC I — Ton profil (51-60)

### 51. Tell me about yourself.
> "I'm Calvin, a final-year Master's student at SKEMA in Paris, focusing on
> market finance. What got me into trading was one concrete thing: I built a lab
> where I model a macro shock and measure the P&L of a hedged book on real data.
> What it taught me is that **timing matters more than direction** — exiting at
> the model peak versus holding to the stop was worth about **six hundred
> thousand dollars** on a twenty-five million book. I also did multi-asset
> reporting at BPCE Assurances, so I've worked with Bloomberg curves daily."

### 52. ⭐ What is ShockDesk?
> "It's a research desk I built. I publish a **dated** macro forecast — say an
> oil shock — then I trade a multi-asset book against it and score myself
> afterwards. The point isn't the P&L, it's the **discipline**: the forecast
> function is point-in-time so the backtest can't see the future, the stop is set
> **ex-ante**, and the **misses are published** next to the hits."

> 💡 **Focus.** Ne commence **jamais** par le P&L. Commencer par la méthode
> désamorce le scepticisme : ton interlocuteur sait que 90 % des projets
> étudiants sont des courbes flatteuses. Toi, tu vends l'**auditabilité**.

### 53. Why should I believe your numbers?
> "Because the failures are on the same page as the wins. On the oil book, HYG
> and TLT both went the wrong way and they're marked as misses publicly. And
> `get_forecast` only returns revisions published **before** the bar — so there's
> no look-ahead. You can check it."

### 54. Your alpha is negative — why should I be impressed?
> "You're right, and it's on the page: the book made 1.32 percent while the S&P
> did 3.05. But the book isn't built to beat the index, it's a **shock hedge** —
> beta is minus 0.05. Judging it against a rising S&P is like judging insurance
> in a year with no fire. What I'd judge it on is **eight basis points of
> drawdown**. That said, I should benchmark it against a long-vol book, and
> that's my next change."

> 💡 **Focus.** La séquence gagnante : **reconnaître → recadrer → annoncer le
> correctif**. Ne dis jamais « ce n'est pas grave ». On teste ta réaction sous
> contradiction, pas ton résultat.

### 55. Is this real money?
> "No — it's a backtest on real market data, and I'd never present it as
> anything else. The forecasts are timestamped before the window so there's no
> look-ahead, but it hasn't traded live. That's the honest limit."

### 56. Why Deutsche Bank?
> "Two reasons. Positioning: DB is the **number one European FIC house**, and
> the strategy is explicitly to be the global alternative to US banks — most
> European players are consolidating regionally. And momentum: FIC was **9.6
> billion in 2025, up 13 percent**, with a record last quarter driven by rates
> and credit."

### 57. Why rates rather than equities?
> "Rates is where a view has to be **expressed precisely**. In equities you can
> be roughly right on direction. In rates you pick the point on the curve, the
> instrument, the carry. I like that the thinking is constrained."

### 58. Why not corporate finance, given your Master's?
> "Because I found out what I actually enjoy is being **marked to market every
> day**. In corporate finance you're right in eighteen months. On a desk you're
> right or wrong by the close, and you have to say why. That feedback loop is
> what I want."

> 💡 **Focus.** Ta formation Corporate Finance **sera** questionnée. Cette
> réponse la transforme en choix assumé plutôt qu'en défaut à excuser. Ne
> t'excuse jamais de ton parcours.

### 59. What's your weakness?
> "Speaking markets in English. I read and write it fine, but I'd never
> practised talking about a book out loud under pressure — so I've been drilling
> it daily this week. It's improving, but I'm not going to pretend it's already
> solved."

### 60. Do you have any questions for me?
> "Yes. I keep finding that getting the direction right matters less than
> getting the **timing** right. On a desk, when a macro view is correct but
> early — is that a sizing decision, a stop discipline, or do you just carry
> the position?"

> 💡 **Focus.** **Ne termine jamais par « non ».** Cette question fait trois
> choses : elle prouve que tu construis quelque chose, qu'elle mesure tes
> erreurs, et elle pose un vrai problème de desk. Si on enchaîne par *"what's
> the desk?"* — **tu pitches sur invitation**, ce qui n'a rien à voir.

# Les 5 phrases de secours

| Situation | À dire |
|---|---|
| Répéter | *"Sorry, could you repeat that?"* |
| Gagner 3 secondes | *"That's a good question — let me think."* |
| Tu ne sais pas | *"I don't know that one yet. Can you walk me through it?"* |
| Te corriger | *"Actually, let me correct myself."* |
| Conclure | *"That's how I'd approach it."* |

> **« I don't know » dit calmement est un atout.** Un junior qui invente est
> dangereux sur un desk. Un junior qui dit « pas encore, montrez-moi » est
> formable — et c'est ce qu'on recrute.

## Les 3 pièges de prononciation

**bond** /bond/ — pas « bonde » · **yield** /yeeld/ — pas « yeld » ·
**coupon** /koo-pon/. Et *basis point* se dit **« bip »**.
